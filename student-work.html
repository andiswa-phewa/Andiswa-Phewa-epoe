01 / Student projectSafety app walkthrough

Safer PWA

Safer is a women’s safety and empowerment application concept developed by students. Its focus is on helping women access safety support quickly and intuitively, with an SOS feature at the centre of the experience. The project encourages students to think about designing technology for sensitive, real-world challenges where clarity and responsiveness matter.

Key features and student learning

  • SOS support as a prominent action for someone needing urgent assistance.
  • A safety-first interface designed around women’s security and empowerment.
  • Mobile-focused navigation that makes key actions simple to find.
  • An opportunity to explore accessible interaction design, privacy, safety scenarios and user-centred development.

02 / Student projectMentorship app walkthrough

HerMentor

HerMentor is a student-designed mentorship and career-development application. The prototype goes beyond a single screen to illustrate how a learner and a mentor might move through registration, profile development, matching, sessions and longer-term progress tracking.

Features showcased in the student demonstration

  • Welcome, sign-up and profile setup with interests, skills and career goals.
  • Home dashboard, career guidance and mentor discovery/matching journeys.
  • Mentoring sessions with proposed meeting outcomes, action items and follow-up tasks.
  • Goal milestones and progress views, plus a separate mentor dashboard for requests and mentee management.
  • Profile settings and light/dark appearance concepts.

03 / Student projectLive classroom tool

ClassPulse

ClassPulse is a student-developed classroom intervention system designed to support lecturers during live classes. Students can indicate when they do not understand a topic or explanation, helping the lecturer identify where additional support is needed and respond with a timely teaching intervention.

How ClassPulse supports teaching and learning

  • Allows students to signal areas of confusion or difficulty while a class is in progress.
  • Helps the lecturer recognise when a topic needs another explanation or demonstration.
  • Supports targeted interventions, such as revisiting a concept, providing another example or adjusting the pace.
  • Creates a practical feedback loop between student understanding and lecturer response during a lesson.
  • Demonstrates how a browser-based student project can address a real classroom challenge.
